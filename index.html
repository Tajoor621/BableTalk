Android and iOS · on the device

No more language barrier.

Two people. One phone. Each turn written down, translated, and spoken only after a person has read it.

BableTalk is a face-to-face conversation assistant. You choose a language for each person. They take turns at the phone microphone. The app transcribes the turn and translates it on the device, then shows both languages together.

Speech and translation models may need a connection the first time you prepare them. The conversation does not ask for an account or an API key, and it does not call a cloud speech or translation service.

View the source See a turn

Development build. It is not a certified interpreter, and the on-device checklist is still open. There is no App Store or Play Store listing.

At a glance

  • On the phone

    After the models are prepared, recognition and translation run in the app. The translate step does not download a model and does not call a cloud translator.

  • No account

    There is no sign-in and no API key. The first speech-model download, and translation packs on Wi-Fi, are separate from the conversation.

  • You approve the words

    Stop & translate shows both languages. You can correct the recognised words and translate again. Recording stops before anything is played.

  • Yours to keep or delete

    Audio for a turn stays in memory. Transcripts live in a private database on the device. Sharing happens when you export.

01

A turn, from start to playback

The phone is shared. Both people use its microphone, including when headphones are plugged in. Headphones are for listening. The app asks you to name the speaker, so it does not guess who talked.

  1. Prepare the models once

    In Models, download the speech models. That pack is about 62 MB and is checked by size and SHA-256. Then prepare the language pair. Translation packs download on Wi-Fi.

  2. Choose the two languages

    A is your language. B is the other person’s. English, Urdu, and Arabic can be paired with each other. Pashto can be transcribed, and the app will say that translation is unavailable.

  3. Let one person speak

    Start a session, then tap B speaks or A speaks. You can type a turn when speaking is awkward. A phone call, a locked screen, or leaving the app stops the recording.

  4. Read it, then play it

    Tap Stop & translate. Correct the words if they are wrong, then translate again. Tap Play translation when the line is right. The reply aimed at you can play through headphones. Your reply plays on the phone speaker. If the private headphone route fails, the app reports an error.

  5. Save, export, or delete

    End the session to finish the record. Open Sessions to export JSON or text, or to delete it. An interrupted session stays in that list so you can see it.

02

Languages on the device

Recognition uses a quantised multilingual Whisper base model, with Silero voice detection in front of it. Translation among English, Urdu, and Arabic uses on-device ML Kit. Pashto is a separate, narrower path.

English

Speech and translation

  • Pairs with Urdu and with Arabic.
  • A developer installer can still use the earlier English-only speech model. The in-app download uses the multilingual model.

اردو

Urdu · speech and translation

  • Pairs with English and with Arabic.
  • Short or mixed Urdu and Hindi can be ambiguous. Confirm the turn before playback.

العربية

Arabic · speech and translation

  • Pairs with English and with Urdu.
  • An optional dialect candidate can appear after at least 25 words from the same speaker. The original Arabic is kept.
  • Colloquial wording may need a correction, or a rephrase in Modern Standard Arabic.

پښتو

Pashto · experimental transcription

  • Translation and spoken playback are unavailable. The app says so.
  • Treat the transcript as a draft for a person to read, and expect to check it closely.

03

What stays on the phone

Ask the other person before you record or save their words. The conversation path is built to keep speech and translation on the device. A network check on a real phone is still part of the open device checklist.

During a conversation

  • There is no account to create and no API key to paste in.
  • Audio for the turn is held in memory. It is not written as a recording.
  • The translate call does not start a model download.
  • System voices already on the phone speak the translation. A missing voice leaves the text on screen. There is no speech-synthesis key.

What is stored

  • Transcripts sit in SQLite on the device. Android backup is off. On iOS the database is kept out of backup, with the system’s file protection.
  • The app does not add its own database encryption. A device passcode matters.
  • Export JSON and Export text are explicit. They open the system share sheet.
  • The live view shows the latest 500 turns. The saved-session list shows the latest 200. The database keeps the session record.

Model setup can use the network: speech files from their published URLs, and translation packs over Wi-Fi. ML Kit’s own terms cover that SDK’s operational metadata. The app is written so a transcript is not sent to a network translation endpoint. Confirm that on a device before you rely on it.

04

Honest limits

BableTalk is a development application. The physical-device gate has not been passed. Later features are in the build for testing. They are not a certificate of accuracy, battery life, or headset behaviour.

In this build

  • Turn-by-turn speech, typed turns, correction, and sentence-by-sentence playback.
  • Private headphone output for the translation aimed at you, and the phone speaker for your reply. You can choose the speaker for your own listening.
  • Optional audio language identification. Manual language choice works without it. The optional pack is about 974 MB and its licence is CC-BY-NC-4.0.
  • Optional Arabic dialect candidates from a build-generated model. A missing pack leaves translation available. Urdu, Pashto, and English have no dialect labels here.
  • Controls expose button, tab, and radio roles, with labels on the main inputs. Testing with a screen reader on a phone is still open.

Still open

  • It is not a certified interpreter. Confirm names, amounts, dates, negation, and mixed-language turns.
  • No accuracy figure is published for this app. A model card’s score is not BableTalk’s score.
  • A confident language mismatch holds the translation for review. The session pair stays as you set it.
  • Targets for a short wait and a one-hour session are goals. They are not measured results.
  • Android 12 and newer can select a communication device. Android 7 through 11 in this build support explicit wired output. Older Bluetooth routing still needs device-specific work.
  • There is no browser version of the assistant. This page is the project site.

The checklist for a real phone is docs/DEVICE_ACCEPTANCE.md. A record of what has been checked in development, and what has not, is in docs/VALIDATION.md.

05

Build it from the source

There is no store listing and no hosted demo of the conversation. You build the native app. Source checkout needs Node 22.11 or newer. Android uses JDK 17. iOS needs a Mac with a current Xcode, CocoaPods, and iOS 16 or newer.

Read the setup

Install, tests, model commands, the Arabic dialect pack, and the offline Android variant are written up in the repository README.

Android test artifact

When the native workflow succeeds, it uploads a development-test APK. The artifact is kept for seven days. It is signed with the public template key. Replace that signing and finish device checks before you share a build as a release.
